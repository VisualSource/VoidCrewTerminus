Aux Fighter — System Design

Design VoidCrewTerminus mod Not scheduled Pre-flight required

Technical companion to aux-fighter-rfc.html. Covers the latch state machine, the craft, multiplayer authority, patch surfaces and the phased build. Vocabulary is defined in handoff/CONTEXT.md; this document assumes it.

Context

The Aux Fighter is the mod's third feature area after the Upgrade Forge and the Leech Carrier encounter. It shares their conventions — per-run state, host-arbitrated decisions, VoidManager ModMessage transport, mod-side overlay rather than prefab replacement — and departs from exactly one of them, deliberately, for flight control latency (see Multiplayer Sync).

Sequencing This design depends on the same net layer as Phase 8, which is code-complete but only partially verified in-game. Four of seven 2-client tests have evidence; T4, T6 and T7 do not. The net layer was also refactored behind IForgeTransport on 2026-08-14 and has not been re-verified since. Building the Aux Fighter's sync on top of an unverified layer means any failure has two possible causes. Finish Phase 8 verification first.

System Summary

A CellModule holds a mod-authored SpaceCraft by reparenting it, exactly as a vanilla Turret is parented to the ship it sits on. Module power drives a five-state latch machine whose transitions are the launch and recovery mechanics. A pilot enters through a TakeoverChair-derived cockpit seat, which transfers the craft's PhotonView ownership to them so flight is locally responsive; every consequential state change stays host-arbitrated over ModMessage. Enemy attention is governed by a small SignatureRadius rather than by damage rules.

Fighter Bay

A CellModule subclass following the Upgrade Forge's pattern: a mod-authored prefab loaded through the RuntimeAssets pipeline, attached at runtime by a Harmony postfix on module instantiation, with named anchor transforms for the capture volume, the dock point and the panel.

It owns: the latch state machine, the capture volume, the exterior hardpoint, the Docking Collar alignment, the alarm, the panel interactions, and the bay's stocked-or-empty status. It does not own the Fighter's flight, weapons or fuel burn — those live on the craft.

Geometry The Fighter attaches outside the hull. Prefab anchors needed: a CaptureVolume trigger, a Hardpoint transform carrying position and orientation (the Fighter is aligned to it, so its rotation is load-bearing), a Collar transform marking the interior side of the pass-through, and a Panel anchor. The Forge's PlaceAtAnchor already solves base-pivot alignment against a rotated, non-uniformly scaled anchor with quaternions — reuse that maths rather than rediscovering it.

Latch State Machine

Five states, driven by PowerDrain.IsOn and by occupancy. Power transitions come from the vanilla power system; the Forge's RandomShutoffBehavior already demonstrates hooking PowerDrain.IsOn.OnChange and using CellModule.TurnOff()/TurnOn().

StateEnter whenBehaviourExits to
DarkUnpowered, bay emptyInert.Grab State (power on)
Grab StatePowered, hardpoint emptyReaching. Nothing happens until an Aux Fighter crosses MaxRange.Capturing (Fighter in range) · Dark (power off)
CapturingFighter inside MaxRange, outside CatchRadiusBay drags the Fighter in and rotates it toward the hardpoint orientation. Interruptible — the pilot can Break Away under thrust.Latched (crosses CatchRadius) · Grab State (Break Away) · Dark (power off)
LatchedFighter clamped, poweredFighter parented to the ship, inert, Collar aligned so the cockpit is reachable from inside. Stable state.Releasing · Release Grace
ReleasingPower cut deliberately at the panelLatch drops immediately. This is the launch.Dark
Release GracePower lost involuntarily while LatchedAlarm plus countdown. Restoring power returns to Latched; expiry drops the latch.Latched (restored) · Dark (expiry)
Design note Distinguishing Releasing from Release Grace is the crux of the module. Both are "power went away", but one is a crew decision and the other is an accident, and they must feel completely different. The panel action is the only route into Releasing; every other cause of power loss routes into Release Grace.

Capture Envelope

Capture is a process, not a snap, and it is interruptible up to a point. All four parameters exist on CarryableAttractor already.

ZoneVanilla fieldBehaviourBreak Away?
Outside> MaxRangeNothing. The bay is reaching but cannot touch the Fighter.n/a
PullMaxRange → CatchRadiusBay drags the Fighter in at _pullVelocity and rotates it toward the hardpoint's orientation.Yes — out-thrust the pull.
Committed< CatchRadiusCapture completes. Alignment finishes, Collar lines up, bay Latches.No. Point of no return.
HysteresisBufferRangeKeeps a Fighter that has just broken away from being re-grabbed on the same frame.—
Why interruptible Auto-capture on proximity was rejected in the RFC precisely because being yanked out of a dogfight by your own ship would feel awful. The Capture Envelope keeps the convenience — the bay does the attitude match, the pilot just flies at it — while leaving the pilot an out until they are genuinely committed. BufferRange is what stops a break-away turning into a yo-yo.
Free synergy AttractorMaxRange and AttractorPullVelocity are StatTypes, and the Upgrade Forge's Utility group already boosts both (ForgeModuleState.BuildMods), as does the existing utility_extended_manipulators perk. Tagging the Fighter Bay as a Utility module means forging it widens the Capture Envelope and speeds the pull with no new code — a forged bay recovers a Fighter from further out and faster.

Aux Fighter

A mod-authored SpaceCraft. Dock state is expressed by parenting, following Turret:

ConditionParentSpaceCraftMeaning
LatchedThe player shipDocked. Inherits faction, rides the hull, flight disabled.
ReleasedItselfIndependent. Flight enabled. Vanilla already treats self-parenting as independence.

Cockpit & Entry

A TakeoverChair subclass on the Fighter's cockpit anchor. Entry is by interaction prompt, identical to taking over a turret: the pilot holds the prompt at the bay and the vanilla chair machinery seats them. The character never traverses the hull.

This is worth stating plainly because it removes a whole class of risk. There is no pathing from ship interior onto a hull-exterior craft, no doorway, no pressurisation handoff, and no chance of a character falling through geometry at the boundary. The Docking Collar is an alignment, not a passage — its job is to put the cockpit adjacent to the module so the prompt is offered, and to gate boarding on Latched by geometry rather than by a rule.

Exit is the vanilla chair exit, returning the pilot to the bay interior. Boarding therefore never involves EVA or pressurisation, which confines EVA strictly to the failure path where it belongs.

Riding the craft is the chair's job, not ours. TakeoverChair carries a public SpacePlatform Platform, and SittingInChair calls SetMovingPlatform(chair.Platform.transform) when the pilot sits. Setting our cockpit chair's Platform to the Aux Fighter therefore makes the pilot ride it via the engine's own path — already network-synced, already storing position platform-relative. The requirement this creates is concrete: the Fighter needs a SpacePlatform on the MovingPlatform layer.

Sitting triggers the vanilla RequestSitInChair RPC, which transfers PhotonView ownership to the sitter. That is precisely the ownership model the flight code needs, and it arrives for free.

WorksWithJetpack should be true: the pilot must be able to sit while wearing a jetpack, because ejection puts them straight into EVA and a pilot who had to remove their jetpack to fly would be ejecting to certain death.

Flight Model & Camera

Neither ShipEngine nor ShipExternalCamera can be reused — the first is a CellModule bound to the ship's grid, the second resolves ClientGame.Current.PlayerShip in Start(). But both should be modelled closely on how they work, so the Fighter feels like it belongs to this game rather than bolted on. What follows is the vanilla behaviour, read out of the decompile, and what to copy.

How ShipEngine actually flies the ship

Owner-gated integration in FixedUpdate, with force and torque applied separately:

MechanismVanillaCopy?
Named input channelsThrustInputs/TorqueInputs are Dictionary<string, Vector3> summed each tick. "PlayerInput" is one key; cruise control and debug are others.Yes. Cheap, and it makes assists or autopilot additive later rather than a rewrite.
Owner gateif (IsPowered && photonView.AmOwner) wraps both ApplyForce and ApplyTorque.Yes. Exactly the split-authority model — the pilot owns the view, so the pilot integrates.
Per-axis power vectorEngineThrustPower = (50, 50, 500) — forward is 10× lateral. Boost has its own vector.Structure yes, values no. A fighter wants a far flatter ratio.
Stat-scaledScaled again by StrifePower, ElevationPower, ForwardPower, YawPower, EnginePower — all registered StatTypes.Yes. Registering stats is what makes the Fighter forge-upgradeable, exactly as the bay's attractor stats already are.
dt-scaled AddForceAddForce(force * Time.fixedDeltaTime, worldSpace: true, userInputsThrust) — the third argument tells the platform whether the player is actively inputting.Yes, including the input flag; it is what lets damping distinguish coasting from driving.
The big one The player ship is not 6DOF. It is 2.5D. ApplyForce builds its heading from Quaternion.FromToRotation(Vector3.forward, Vector3.ProjectOnPlane(transform.forward, Vector3.up)) — the forward vector flattened onto the horizontal plane — and EngineTorquePower = (0, 50, 0) is yaw only. There is no pitch and no roll. Vertical movement is a separate world-axis elevation input, not a consequence of pointing the nose up.

This is the one place a faithful copy is probably wrong. A capital ship that yaws and elevates like a boat reads as weighty and correct; a fighter that cannot pitch or roll will read as broken, and the Fighter's whole job is dogfighting Drones that move freely. The recommendation is therefore: copy the architecture exactly, change the axes deliberately — keep named channels, the owner gate, per-axis power vectors, stat registration and dt-scaled application, but populate TorquePower on all three axes and drop the ProjectOnPlane flattening so thrust follows the nose.

Prototype question Worth building both and flying them: full 6DOF, versus the ship's 2.5D model with pitch added. The second may feel more consistent with the game and be less disorienting for a player who has only ever flown the ship. This is the single question the throwaway prototype exists to answer.

How ShipExternalCamera actually works

A Cinemachine rig with an acceleration-driven trailing offset:

  • Cinemachine3rdPersonFollow drives the shot. The mod does not need to hand-roll a follow camera — Cinemachine ships with the game.
  • Acceleration offset, not velocity offset. Move() reads engine.AppliedThrust (the summed input, not the resulting speed) × accelerationOffsetMultiplier (0.5), rotates it into the ship's heading, and lerps toward it at Time.deltaTime * 3f. The anchor is then pushed backward by that offset, so the camera trails when you accelerate and settles when you coast. Reading input rather than velocity is why it responds the instant you touch the throttle.
  • Free-look with a pitch clamp — RotateCamera(Vector2 mouseDelta) with RotationSensitivity and XRotationLimit.
  • Yaw-only re-alignment on toggle — Quaternion.LookRotation(Vector3.ProjectOnPlane(parent.forward, Vector3.up), Vector3.up), consistent with the 2.5D flight model.
Do not miss this ShipExternalCamera implements IKinematicObject and registers with Opsive's SimulationManager, so Move() runs inside the character-controller simulation step — not Update or LateUpdate. Since our pilot is an Opsive character riding the Fighter as a moving platform, the Fighter's camera must do the same or it will jitter against the seated character. This is the kind of detail that is invisible in design and obvious in play.

What the Fighter's implementation therefore is

  1. A flight controller with summed named input channels, integrating in FixedUpdate, gated on photonView.AmOwner.
  2. Per-axis thrust and torque power vectors, scaled by registered StatTypes so the Forge can upgrade them, applied via AddForce/AddTorque scaled by Time.fixedDeltaTime with the active-input flag.
  3. Torque on all three axes, and thrust following the nose — the deliberate deviation.
  4. A Cinemachine3rdPersonFollow rig whose anchor carries an acceleration-driven trailing offset lerped at ~3/sec, registered as an IKinematicObject.

Weapons & Signature

Outgoing. Damage applies only when the target resolves to a Drone. Implemented as a target-type check at the damage application point, mirroring the existing EnemyScalingHelpers.IsEnemyFaction pattern — a small pure predicate, unit-testable without a game running.

Incoming. No damage rules at all. The Fighter is given a small SignatureRadius, and vanilla target selection does the rest:

  • Player ship in range → it scores better, so large ships engage it and ignore the Fighter.
  • Player ship out of range and the Fighter close → the Fighter becomes the best available target.
  • Per-enemy-type preference is a tuning knob on top, not new logic.

Loss & Ejection

One ejection path serves both the manual bail and destruction, which keeps the code and the fiction aligned.

  1. Pilot leaves the chair (vanilla chair exit).
  2. Character is placed outside the Fighter in EVA.
  3. Survival is governed entirely by existing systems: oxygen, jetpack fuel, distance, and swarm EVA intercepts via InterceptEVAModule.

Destruction runs the same path automatically before despawning the craft. A destroyed Fighter empties the bay; the crew must buy a replacement.

Jump Interaction

Driven off VoidJumpSystem.StateChanged. Host-arbitrated — the host owns the outcome and broadcasts it.

Jump stateFighter releasedOutcome
SpinningUpYesWarning to the crew. Still recoverable — this is the window to get home.
TravellingStableRelease attemptedFighter destroyed. Pilot dies, respawns at a clone tube.
Jump completesYesFighter left behind and lost. Any pilot aboard dies and respawns at a clone tube.

Economy & Panel

The panel is a mod interactable on the bay, offering four actions against GameSessionSuppliesManager alloys — the same API the Forge's Alloy Terminal uses, including its host-only spend behaviour and the existing client→host request hop.

ActionPreconditionEffect
Buy FighterBay empty, Fighter Cap not reached, alloys sufficientSpawns a Latched Fighter.
RefuelLatchedAlloys to fuel.
RepairLatched, damagedAlloys to hull.
ReleaseLatchedCuts power deliberately → Releasing. The launch.

The Fighter Cap of 2 is enforced at purchase, host-side, counting live Fighters across all bays.

Architecture

Following the deep-module discipline applied to the Forge, the Unity-entangled parts are kept thin and the decisions are kept pure and testable.

ModuleInterfaceTestable?
Latch state machineCurrent, OnPowerChanged(bool), OnFighterEntered(), Tick(dt), RequestRelease()Yes — pure. Every transition, including grace expiry, unit-testable with no game running.
Target eligibilityCanDamage(target) : boolYes — pure predicate over type/faction.
Fighter economyCanBuy(liveCount, alloys) : ResultYes — pure. Cap and cost logic.
Jump outcomeResolveJump(jumpState, fighterState) : OutcomeYes — pure decision table.
Bay behaviourMonoBehaviour; capture volume, anchors, alarmNo — playtest.
Flight controllerMonoBehaviour; input, integration, cameraNo — playtest.

The lesson from the Forge's net layer applies directly: put the decisions behind pure interfaces and leave only scene manipulation in the MonoBehaviours. The latch machine in particular is a state machine with a timer — precisely the kind of thing that is miserable to verify by launching a game twice.

Multiplayer Sync

The mod's convention is host-arbitrated throughout. This feature takes one deliberate, bounded exception.

ConcernOwnerWhy
Position, rotation, thrust, firingPilot's clientFlight cannot round-trip through the host and remain flyable. Ownership arrives free via TakeoverChair's existing transfer, and transform replication is what every networked SpaceCraft already does.
Latch state, Release GraceHostConsequential. Broadcast over ModMessage.
Purchase, Fighter Cap, fuel, repairHostSpends shared resources; a client must not be able to conjure a Fighter.
Destruction, ejection, jump outcomeHostDecides whether a player dies.
Trust boundary A misbehaving client can make its Fighter fly strangely. It cannot conjure Fighters, fake a dock, exceed the Fighter Cap, refuse to die on a jump, or spend alloys it does not have. That is the right line, and it is the same line CONTEXT.md already draws — the exception is scoped to presentation of motion, not to state.

Late joiners need bay state, stocked status and any live Fighter's state, following the existing SendCatchUpTo pattern. Host migration re-asserts bay state exactly as OnMasterClientSwitched already does for forge state.

Pre-Flight Verification

Required before any code, in order. The reference-only NuGet DLLs have empty method bodies, so this must be done against a live install with ilspycmd.

  1. Character on a manoeuvring craft. No longer a feasibility question — the mechanism exists (TakeoverChair.Platform → SetMovingPlatform) and vanilla explicitly handles a seated player off the ship's platform. What remains is narrower: give the Fighter a SpacePlatform on the MovingPlatform layer, seat a pilot, and confirm the character holds up under fighter-scale accelerations, which are far harsher than the ship's. A quality question, not a go/no-go.
  2. SignatureRadius is settable per instance and actually consulted by TargetSelector at runtime.
  3. Damage application point — the correct member to gate the Drone-only rule.
  4. TakeoverChair subclassing from a mod assembly, and whether ownership transfer survives it.
  5. Mod-authored SpaceCraft instantiation over Photon via the RuntimeAssets path.
  6. Cinemachine availability from a mod assembly — the game ships it and ShipExternalCamera uses Cinemachine3rdPersonFollow, but confirm a mod can construct a rig at runtime rather than only via an authored prefab.
  7. ParentSpaceCraft reassignment at runtime — is it safe to flip after spawn, and does faction/hostiles follow?
